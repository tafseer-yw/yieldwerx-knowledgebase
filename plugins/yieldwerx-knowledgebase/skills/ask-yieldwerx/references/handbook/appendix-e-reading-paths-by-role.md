# Appendix E — Reading Paths by Role

This handbook is a single source of truth for the whole company, but no one needs to read it the same way. Use these paths as a starting point; every reader benefits from Chapters 0–2, which frame *why the domain matters*.

**New engineering hire (any discipline).** Read cover to cover, in order — that is exactly what the handbook is designed for. Do the quizzes honestly. Pay special attention to Chapter 5 (the statistics survival kit) and Chapters 6–7 (data files and architecture), which underpin everything technical.

**QA / SQA engineer.** After Chapters 0–7, prioritize every chapter’s *Bug-Hunting & Hardening Tips* section, the per-module chapters (9–13), the Rule Engine (15), and Appendices A (worked answers), C (known-gaps register), and the invariant checklist in Chapter 14. Build the standing health-check job described there.

**Software developer / platform engineer.** Focus on Chapters 6–8 and 15 (architecture, data flow, the Rule Engine design), plus each module’s data model and cross-module dependencies. The *Bug-Hunting & Hardening Tips* double as a secure-coding and edge-case checklist.

**Product manager / business analyst.** Read Chapters 1–3 (economics, players, lifecycle) and 14 (turning domain knowledge into journeys and PRDs), then skim each module’s opening and its *Global Trends & the Bigger Picture* section to understand customer value and roadmap context.

**Upper management / leadership.** Read Chapter 1 (why yield is a money number), Chapter 2 (where yieldWerx fits and its ROI story), and the *Global Trends & the Bigger Picture* section of every chapter — together they give the strategic picture (market size, AI/ML direction, chiplets and advanced packaging, traceability and regulatory pressure) without requiring the technical depth. The *Summary* and *Never Forget* boxes provide a fast executive digest of each topic.

**Sales / customer success / field application.** Chapters 1–3 for the value narrative and vocabulary, then each module’s *purpose* paragraph and *Global Trends* section so you can speak credibly to customers about what the platform does and why it matters.

**Technical writer / trainer.** The whole book is your source; the recurring section structure, the figures with their “How to read this figure” explainers, and the quizzes are ready-made training scaffolding (the companion NotebookLM video prompts turn each chapter into a lesson).

A shared vocabulary is the real deliverable: when an executive, a PM, a developer, and a QA engineer all use “yield,” “bin,” “PAT,” and “genealogy” to mean exactly the same thing, the company moves faster. That alignment — not any single chapter — is what makes this a one-stop shop.
